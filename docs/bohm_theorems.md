## prompt

what are the theorems tied to bohmian mechanics

## answer

Several foundational theorems, lemmas, and mathematical proofs establish how Bohmian mechanics (de Broglie–Bohm pilot-wave theory) reproduces standard quantum mechanics or behaves in non-equilibrium conditions.
## 1. The POVM Theorem (Measurement Formalism)
The [POVM (Positive Operator-Valued Measure) Theorem](https://www.mdpi.com/1099-4300/27/4/391) is a critical cornerstone of Bohmian mechanics. Because Bohmian mechanics doesn't treat operators as fundamental "observables" (only particle positions are real), it must mathematically prove why standard textbook quantum operators arise. [1, 2, 3] 

* 
* The Core: It proves that under the assumption of quantum equilibrium, the statistical outcomes of any experiment performed on a sub-system can be completely described by a POVM acting on the sub-system's Hilbert space. [1] 
* Significance: It justifies the standard quantum measurement framework, deriving the existence of self-adjoint operators (and standard Projection-Valued Measures/PVMs) strictly from the deterministic trajectory analysis of the combined system and measurement apparatus. [1] 
* 

## 2. Valentini’s H-Theorem (Quantum Non-Equilibrium)
As a subquantum equivalent to Boltzmann's classical H-theorem, Valentini's H-Theorem explains the origin of the Born rule. [4] 

* 
* The Core: It states that if a system starts in "quantum non-equilibrium"—where the actual spatial probability density ρ ≠ |ψ|²—the coarse-grained subquantum entropy functional H will dynamically decrease over time. [4] 
* Significance: It proves that a system will naturally relax toward an equilibrium state where ρ = |ψ|², mathematically formalizing why we observe Born rule probabilities in our current universe.
* 

## 3. The Equivariance Theorem
The Equivariance Theorem ensures that Bohmian mechanics maintains empirical consistency over time.

* 
* The Core: It proves that if the probability density distribution of initial particle positions matches the quantum distribution (ρ(x, t₀) = |ψ(x, t₀)|²), then the joint evolution of the Schrödinger equation and the guiding equation guarantees they will match at all future times (ρ(x, t) = |ψ(x, t)|²).
* Significance: This theorem establishes that quantum equilibrium is a stable, preserved state of the universe. [5, 6] 
* 

## 4. No-Signaling Theorem (Signal-Locality)
Even though Bohmian mechanics is explicitly non-local (a change in particle A can instantaneously alter the velocity of entangled particle B across space), it satisfies the No-Signaling Theorem. [2, 6] 

* 
* The Core: It proves that as long as the system remains in quantum equilibrium, the non-local connections at the subquantum layer average out perfectly.
* Significance: This ensures that macroscopic observers cannot utilize Bohmian non-locality to transmit data or signals faster than the speed of light, keeping it fully compliant with special relativity in practical applications. [4, 7] 
* 

## 5. Dürr–Goldstein–Zanghì (DGZ) Typicality Theorems
Formulated by physicists Detlef Dürr, Sheldon Goldstein, and Nino Zanghì, these theorems address the statistical foundations of the theory.

* 
* The Core: Rather than relying purely on subjective probabilities, DGZ proved via mathematical typicality that relative to the universal wave function, the vast majority ("overwhelming typicality") of initial configurations for a subsystem will exhibit Born rule frequencies.
* Significance: It provides a rigorous framework showing that Born-rule probabilities are a objective, typical property of subsystems in a single, unique universe.
*

## prompt

of the five above which is the one most foundational theorem per bohmian mechanics or the most significant if you were to pick only one


## answer

If you have to pick the single most significant and foundational theorem of Bohmian mechanics, it is arguably the Equivariance Theorem. [1, 2] 
While all five serve distinct purposes, the Equivariance Theorem is the mathematical bridge that allows Bohmian mechanics to exist as a viable, predictive physics theory.
## Why Equivariance is the Most Significant
The entire premise of Bohmian mechanics is that point particles move along precise, deterministic trajectories guided by a wave function ($\psi$). However, for this theory to actually represent our universe, it must replicate the statistical predictions of standard quantum mechanics—specifically, the Born rule ($\rho = \vert{}\psi\vert{}^2$). [1, 3, 4, 5] 
The Equivariance Theorem mathematically guarantees this connection by proving that the two foundational equations of the theory are perfectly compatible over time. It dictates that: [2, 6] 

   1. If you start a system in "quantum equilibrium" where the particle density exactly equals the quantum distribution ($\rho = \vert{}\psi\vert{}^2$),
   2. The guiding equation and the Schrödinger equation will work in lockstep to ensure the particles stay distributed exactly at $\rho = \vert{}\psi\vert{}^2$ for all eternity. [2, 6] 

Without this theorem, a system might match standard quantum predictions for a fraction of a second before spiraling into a completely un-quantum distribution. Equivariance ensures empirical equivalence. [5] 
## How the Other Four Rely on It
The remaining four theorems are essentially deep explorations, consequences, or justifications of the Equivariance Theorem:

* 
* The POVM Theorem: You cannot mathematically derive standard textbook operators or "observables" unless you first assume the system is in a stable state of quantum equilibrium—which requires Equivariance. [7, 8] 
* The No-Signaling Theorem: Bohmian mechanics is explicitly non-local. The only reason it doesn't violate Einstein's special relativity at a macroscopic level is that the non-local signals average out to zero. That averaging out only happens when the particles are distributed according to $\rho = \vert{}\psi\vert{}^2$ (Equivariance). [1, 4, 9] 
* DGZ Typicality & Valentini's H-Theorems: These are philosophical and mathematical justifications for why the universe started in equilibrium in the first place. But what they are trying to explain the origin of is the very equilibrium state that Equivariance preserves. [1, 10] 
* 

## The Summary Verdict
If you subtract any of the other theorems, Bohmian mechanics loses an explanation or a sub-framework. If you subtract the Equivariance Theorem, the theory immediately contradicts standard quantum mechanics, invalidates the Born rule, and falls apart entirely.
